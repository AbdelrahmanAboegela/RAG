## TL;DR
LatentRAG replaces the natural-language thoughts and sub-queries of an agentic RAG loop with a handful of **latent tokens**, computed in one forward pass and fed straight into the retriever. It matches Search-R1 and AutoRefine accuracy at **about 90% lower latency**.

## 1. Problem and gap
Agentic RAG is slow. Search-R1 takes **16–22×** the inference time of naive RAG on multi-hop QA, and about **90%** of that time goes to autoregressively decoding thoughts and sub-queries. Latent-reasoning work (for example Coconut) shows that hidden states can carry reasoning without decoding tokens, but nobody had connected latent reasoning to *retrieval*.

## 2. Key idea
At each step, append m=4 `<think>` and n=16 `<query>` placeholder tokens and run **one prefill pass**. Their final hidden states *are* the thought and the sub-query. Project the query states into a dense retriever's embedding space and search with them directly.

```
[context] + <think>×4 + <query>×16 ──(1 forward pass)──► H_think, H_query
H_query ─► Proj_ret ─► retriever encoder ─► vector ─► top-k docs ─► append ─► next step
```

## 3. Method
- **Latent retrieval:** v = f_ret(Proj_ret(H_query)), where Proj_ret is a bidirectional self-attention layer plus an FFN, and f_ret is a trainable dense retriever initialised from a pretrained one.
- **Loss = L_gen + λ_ret·L_ret + L_dec**, with λ_ret = 1:
  - *L_gen:* cross-entropy on action tokens and the final answer.
  - *L_ret:* KL divergence between the document-similarity distribution induced by the **text** sub-query (frozen reference retriever) and the one induced by the **latent** sub-query, with temperature β = 0.03. This aligns latent queries with what a text query would have retrieved.
  - *L_dec:* an optional decoder (Proj_τ, Proj_s) reconstructs the text thought and query from the latents, for transparency.
- **Data:** about 170K correct trajectories generated by **Search-R1 and AutoRefine** on NQ + HotpotQA, so this is SFT or distillation, not RL.
- **Training:** Qwen2.5-7B Base with LoRA rank 16, 5 epochs, learning rate 1e-4. It is also scaled to 3B and 14B.

## 4. Results
- **Datasets (7):** NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue, Bamboogle.
- **Baselines:** direct, naive RAG, Iter-RetGen, Search-o1, R1-Searcher, ZeroSearch, DeepRAG, Search-R1, AutoRefine.

| Teacher | Teacher EM / latency | LatentRAG EM / latency |
|---|---|---|
| Search-R1 | 42.47 / 5,372 ms | **43.46 / 593 ms** (−89.0%) |
| AutoRefine | 42.73 / 4,827 ms | **43.79 / 512 ms** (−89.4%) |

(Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B retriever.) Across five retrievers, the latency reduction stays at about 90%, with accuracy within 5% relative. **With the optional decoding turned on,** latency is still 63.3% and 47.4% below the two teachers.

## 5. Limitations
- **(stated)** It is bounded by the teacher trajectories, because it is trained with SFT, not RL.
- **(stated)** An anisotropic embedding space (e5-base-v2) causes a larger drop (−4.0%).
- **(ours)** Latent queries are **not auditable by default**. You cannot inspect or log what was searched without the decoder. That matters for trust and debugging (see [docs/08](../../docs/08-evidence-and-trust.md)).
- **(ours)** The retriever becomes model-specific: you cannot swap in a new embedding model without retraining.

## 6. What changed vs. the previous approach
Agentic RAG no longer has to "talk to itself" in text. The interface between the reasoner and the retriever becomes a **learned vector**, which closes most of the latency gap to single-step RAG.

## 7. Discussion questions
1. What would RL on top of LatentRAG need? What is the action space when queries are continuous vectors?
2. Compare with CLaRa ([arXiv:2511.18659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18659), Apple), which also unifies retrieval and generation in a continuous space. What differs in *what* is latent?
3. Is a 10× latency cut worth losing human-readable queries in a regulated domain?
